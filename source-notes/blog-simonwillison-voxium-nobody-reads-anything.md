## Extracted Claims

### Claim 1: At this company, every category of technical artifact — specs, code, tests, PRDs, tickets, ticket resolutions, and reports — is produced by Claude Code rather than by the people nominally responsible for them
- **Evidence**: First-person, present-tense description from an engineer two weeks into the role, listing artifact types by name.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal (single, anonymous, unverified social-media account)
- **Quote**: "The specs, code, tests, PRDs, tickets, resolution of those tickets, reports, etc., everything is made by Claude Code."
- **Our assessment**: This is the most extreme "generation without differentiation" pattern in the corpus — it is not that code is AI-generated and reviewed by humans who write specs and tickets themselves; every category of written artifact in the software lifecycle is claimed to be AI-generated. If accurate, this removes the "someone still writes the spec/ticket that frames the work" checkpoint that other corpus sources implicitly assume survives even aggressive AI adoption (e.g., Fung's account of Claude-assisted-but-human-authored PRs in `blog-anthropic-ai-native-engineering-org.md`). We cannot verify this is literally true rather than rhetorical emphasis from a frustrated new hire, but it is the claim's core assertion regardless.

### Claim 2: The poster's teammates dislike this workflow, but are compelled to maximize shipped output regardless of that preference
- **Evidence**: First-person observation of team sentiment plus a stated management pressure.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "Nobody on my team likes this. They are being forced to ship as much as they can."
- **Our assessment**: This describes adoption-by-mandate without buy-in — a sharp contrast to the corpus's positive-pattern sources, which describe voluntary or dogfooding-driven adoption (e.g., Fung's "relentlessly dogfood your product" principle in `blog-anthropic-ai-native-engineering-org.md`, Claim 10). "Forced to ship as much as they can" is a throughput mandate with no stated verification counterpart, which is the precise anti-pattern several existing corpus notes warn against (see Cross-References).

### Claim 3: Company management believes pushing code is not the constraint on velocity, yet cannot explain why the team is slow despite that belief
- **Evidence**: First-person report of a recurring statement from "higher management," presented by the poster as evidence of management's confusion.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "I have heard multiple times from higher management that pushing code is not a bottleneck, so why are we slow?"
- **Our assessment**: Read literally, management has correctly identified that code-writing is no longer the constraint — a diagnosis several credible corpus sources independently reach (Fung: "writing code, writing tests, and refactoring rarely slows us down anymore," `blog-anthropic-ai-native-engineering-org.md` Claim 1; Osmani's Faros-sourced review-bottleneck data, `blog-addyosmani-agentic-code-review.md` Claim 2). What the quote suggests management has *not* done is act on that diagnosis: nothing in the account indicates the company redirected effort toward the new bottleneck (verification, review, comprehension) the way Fung and Osmani describe. The confusion ("why are we slow") is consistent with correctly naming the old bottleneck's absence while failing to find or address the new one.

### Claim 4: Engineers at this company are working 12-13 hour days performing minimal supervisory action rather than substantive engineering work
- **Evidence**: First-person estimate of daily working hours and a characterization of what that time is spent doing.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "People are working 12 to 13 hours a day just to press enter."
- **Our assessment**: This is a burnout claim with a specific (if self-estimated, unverified) hours figure, and it inverts the productivity promise of AI-assisted coding: long hours are being spent not on engineering judgment but on a minimal-effort supervisory action ("press enter"). This is thematically adjacent to but mechanically distinct from `blog-simonwillison-the-pressure.md`'s burnout account (Claims 5-6: a curl maintainer's 50+ hour weeks and family strain from an AI-amplified *inbound report volume* he must triage); here the claimed cause of long hours is the opposite mechanism — not an external flood of AI-generated work arriving for a human to process, but an internal mandate to keep operating an all-AI generation pipeline with no time freed up despite the automation.

### Claim 5: No one at the company reviews or reads the Claude Code-generated outputs before they ship
- **Evidence**: First-person, blanket statement of team-wide practice.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "Nobody is reading anything."
- **Our assessment**: This is the single most guide-relevant claim in the source: a first-person account of the "meat proxy" failure mode (`blog-simonwillison-gruhn-meat-proxy.md`) and the "normalization of deviance" erosion of review discipline (`blog-simonwillison-vibe-coding-agentic-engineering.md` Claim 2) taken to a claimed organizational extreme — not one person occasionally relaying unread AI output, but an entire team's stated universal practice. If accurate, this is the terminal case of the "generation is cheap, understanding is not" pattern documented across the corpus (see Cross-References), with no counterbalancing verification layer at all.

### Claim 6: This pattern — all-AI generation with no review — applies uniformly across the entire engineering seniority ladder, with no differentiation between junior and senior roles
- **Evidence**: First-person claim of uniformity across levels, using the company's own leveling ladder (L1-L7) as the frame.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "Everyone, literally everyone, from an L1 to an L7 engineer here is doing the same thing. Talk to Claude."
- **Our assessment**: This directly contradicts the role-differentiation pattern described in the corpus's positive-adoption sources, where senior engineers specifically retain or take on judgment-heavy responsibilities that don't get automated away (Fung: humans retain legal, security, and product-taste review in `blog-anthropic-ai-native-engineering-org.md` Claim 6; Fung's hiring philosophy explicitly deprioritizes raw throughput in favor of "deep systems expertise," Claim 9; Osmani's blast-radius tiering assumes someone with judgment decides the tier, `blog-addyosmani-agentic-code-review.md` Claim 7). If a company's L7 engineers are doing the identical unread-relay task as its L1s, none of that judgment-based differentiation is occurring — which is consistent with, and helps explain, Claim 5's "nobody is reading anything."
