# the eighteen-month recap: AI Engineer, Singapore, May 2026

> An edited transcript (with slides and embedded video) of Geoffrey Huntley's
> AI Engineer Singapore talk, eighteen months after he published the Ralph
> loop technique, arguing "software development now costs less than minimum
> wage" and citing a Cursor-meetup democratization anecdote, an Atlassian
> layoffs-timing coincidence, and a tour-guide anecdote as evidence the shift
> has become mainstream — all first-person assertion, with the free portion
> of the post cutting off behind a subscriber paywall partway through.

## Extracted Claims

### Claim 1: Software development now costs less than a minimum-wage worker's pay

- **Evidence**: Bare first-person assertion in the talk, restated and
  quantified in the linked companion post `ghuntley.com/real/` (27 Feb 2026)
  with a specific hourly figure.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote** (this post): "It's a provocative title because I'm saying that
  software development now costs less than minimum wage."
- **Quote** (`ghuntley.com/real/`, elaborating the same claim seven months
  earlier): "It's now one year later, and the cost of software development
  is $10.42 an hour, which is less than minimum wage and a burger flipper at
  macca's gets paid more than that."
- **Our assessment**: The $10.42/hour figure has no stated methodology in
  either post — no formula, no worked example, no citation for how "the cost
  of software development" was computed. It reads as a compressed talking
  point rather than a calculation the guide could reproduce or check. It is
  directionally consistent with the corpus's existing, more carefully
  sourced claim that code generation itself became "effectively free and
  instant" in 2025 (`blog-simonwillison-charity-majors-code-economics.md`
  Claim 1, Charity Majors, rated `emerging`), but Huntley's claim goes
  further — asserting a specific dollar-per-hour production cost for
  software development as a whole, not just code generation — with
  substantially less evidentiary support than the claim it builds on.

### Claim 2: Computing's democratization is like smartphone cameras making everyone a photographer

- **Evidence**: Bare first-person analogy, stated as the talk's framing
  device for the minimum-wage thesis.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "There was a time when, if you wanted to do photography, you
  had to buy specialized tools. But now everyone's got an iPhone, and
  everyone's a photographer. Think about that. Things have changed."
- **Our assessment**: A reusable, quotable metaphor for the guide's
  democratization narrative, but it is an analogy, not evidence — it asserts
  the mechanism of change (specialized tools becoming ubiquitous consumer
  tools) without establishing that software development has actually
  reached iPhone-camera-level ubiquity or quality-parity. Complements
  Claim 3's coder/engineer distinction from this same author's earlier
  posts: an iPhone makes anyone a photographer in the sense of taking a
  picture, but does not make them a professional photographer, which is the
  same gap Huntley's other posts draw between "software developer" and
  "software engineer."

### Claim 3: The speaker discloses no institutional affiliation before making his claims

- **Evidence**: First-person disclosure, stated as a direct transition into
  the talk's most provocative material.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal (self-disclosed framing, not an industry claim)
- **Quote**: "With that disclaimer out of the way: I do not work for anyone.
  I am completely independent. I do not represent anyone. So this is going
  to get spicy."
- **Our assessment**: This framing is worth noting for the guide precisely
  because it contrasts with `blog-ghuntley-engineer-away-slop.md` Claim 11,
  where this same author discloses a specific employer (Antithesis) two
  months earlier (24 Jul 2026) whose product category his post endorses.
  This talk (per its title, given in May 2026 — before the Antithesis post)
  predates that disclosed employment; the "I do not represent anyone"
  framing should be read as true for this specific talk's timeframe, not as
  a standing claim about the author's later posts.

### Claim 4: The Ralph loop — roughly 18 months old at the time of this talk — required significant "context engineering," not just the loop mechanism itself, to produce real outcomes

- **Evidence**: First-person historical claim with a specific origin-date
  anchor ("roughly a year and a half"), corroborated independently by
  `blog-pragmaticengineer-orosz-loop-engineering.md` Claim 2 (a third-party
  article dating the original Ralph post to 14 Jul 2025 and its virality to
  December 2025).
- **Confidence**: emerging (the origin-date claim is independently
  corroborated; the "quite disruptive" outcome claim itself remains
  first-person assertion)
- **Quote**: "It's been roughly a year and a half since I published the
  technique of allocating memory in a particular way. If you wrap the tool
  calls around another loop, it's just a loop. But there's a lot of science
  in the context engineering needed to actually achieve these outcomes, and
  it's quite disruptive."
- **Our assessment**: This is a compact, later restatement of the loop
  mechanism Huntley's own earlier post (`ghuntley.com/ralph/`, 14 Jul 2025)
  documents in full — see Concrete Artifacts below for the verbatim Bash
  loop and the "one item per loop" / context-window-budget discipline that
  post spells out at length. The "context engineering" framing here is
  consistent with, and postdates, the more detailed technical vocabulary
  documented independently in `blog-addyosmani-loop-engineering.md` and
  `blog-pragmaticengineer-orosz-loop-engineering.md` — this talk does not
  add new mechanism detail, only a compressed one-sentence retrospective on
  mechanism the corpus already has in more depth.

- **Evidence**: First-person eyewitness anecdote naming a specific tool

- **Our assessment**: A single-meetup eyewitness anecdote, not a survey or

  3 — "gated vs malleable") but adds a named, dated, first-hand observation

### Claim 8: A global speaking tour (17 talks across multiple cities) and a tour-guide anecdote in Auckland are offered as evidence that AI awareness/quality-consciousness has reached far outside the tech industry

- **Evidence**: First-person anecdote with a specific count (17 talks) and
  a specific location (Auckland, on a side trip to Hobbiton).
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "I've been traveling around the world for the last three
  months. I think I've given this talk 17 times now in different cities,
  including Auckland... My tour guide asked, 'Geoff, what do you do?' and

  lit up, and he goes, 'Geoff, how good is AI? How good is AI?' What does it

- **Our assessment**: A single anecdote used to support a general-mainstream-
  awareness claim; "token-maxing" applied to a tour guide's casual curiosity
  is a stretch of the term's usual meaning (deliberately maximizing token
  usage/spend in agentic coding), used here loosely as a synonym for "very
  interested in AI capability." Entertaining as a rhetorical device, not
  evidence of anything measurable about adoption depth.

### Claim 10: Model-first, AI-native companies constitute a new competitive category defined by utility-based pricing and near-zero switching costs, which threatens incumbents built on per-seat pricing and technology lock-in

- **Evidence**: First-person assertion in `ghuntley.com/real/` (27 Feb
  2026), elaborated with specific first-hand examples (a Cloudflare D1 to
  PlanetScale Postgres migration performed via a Ralph loop) rather than a
  bare claim.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal
- **Quote**: "Any business model that's based on per-seat pricing, as AI
  starts to rip harder and harder, is going to become much harder to
  maintain headcount within a corporation because model-first companies
  will be coming into business and operating much leaner using
  utility-based pricing. It's a margin game now."
- **Quote** (on lock-in specifically): "Any business thought that revolved
  around the high cost of switching from one technology to another... was a
  form of lock-in. This is provably falsified now... Just last week, I
  migrated from Cloudflare D1 to a PlanetScale Postgres database
  automatically using a Ralph Loop, and it just worked. Full-on data
  migration."
- **Our assessment**: This is a sharper, more mechanism-grounded version of
  the same speculative claim already extracted as `blog-ghuntley-miami-hot-
  takes.md` Claim 6 ("SaaS per-seat economics may become unstable"). That
  claim was framed as an unevidenced prediction ("may become unstable"); this
  post (eight months later) restates it as already happening and backs it
  with one first-hand example (a database migration performed via Ralph).
  One example is not proof of an industry-wide moat collapse, but it is a
  concrete instance where the earlier post gave none. Distinct from, and
  extends, the settled, company-specific pricing-shift claim in
  `blog-simonwillison-product-market-fit.md` Claim 5 (Anthropic and OpenAI's
  own enterprise pricing moves away from per-seat) — that claim is about AI
  vendors' own pricing; this claim is a broader, unevidenced prediction
  about the wider SaaS/incumbent market being disrupted by AI-native
  challengers.

### Claim 11: An anonymous founder reports cutting headcount by two-thirds and having 20 people produce roughly 30x the output of a 60-person team three years earlier

- **Evidence**: Anonymous, unverifiable direct-message quote, relayed by
  Huntley without independent corroboration.
- **Confidence**: anecdotal (single anonymous, unverifiable data point)
- **Quote** (`ghuntley.com/real/`): "Were smaller but effectively cut 2/3rds
  by telling board I wouldn't backfill in May 2023. Best decision as got rid
  of all the people who 'are sick of hearing about ai'. 20ish people now do
  about 30x the output of what having more than 60 did 3 years ago. — an

- **Our assessment**: No company name, no product, no metric definition for
  "output," and no way to verify this claim exists independent of Huntley's
  own relay of a private message. This should not be cited in the guide as
  evidence of a general 30x productivity multiplier — it is a single,
  anonymous, self-reported data point with every incentive (a founder
  justifying a headcount decision after the fact) to overstate the result.

### Claim 12: The Ralph loop, in its purest form, is a single Bash while-loop wrapping a coding agent, disciplined to do exactly one thing per loop iteration

- **Evidence**: Concrete, reproducible technical artifact — a literal shell

- **Confidence**: settled (as a description of the mechanism Huntley himself

- **Our assessment**: This is the mechanism-level grounding this
